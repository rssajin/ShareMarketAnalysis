# Stock Analysis Framework

## 1. Five-layer analysis

Every candidate should pass through:

1. Business — Is this a great business?
2. Financials — Is it financially strong?
3. Growth — Can it compound for 10–15 years?
4. Moat + AI — Can competitors or AI disrupt it?
5. Valuation — Is the current price reasonable?

Only after these layers are evaluated should the system produce an investment suitability result.

## 3. Priority 10

The simplified screen prioritizes:

1. 5Y Sales CAGR >10%
2. 5Y Profit CAGR >12%
3. 5Y EPS CAGR >12%
4. ROCE >15%
5. ROE >15%
6. Debt/Equity <0.5
7. Positive FCF for most years
8. Promoter pledge = 0%
9. Reasonable P/E relative to growth and sector
10. Strong competitive advantage/moat

## 4. Score out of 100

| Dimension | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Business quality | 20 |
| Growth | 20 |
| Profitability | 15 |
| Financial strength | 15 |
| Cash-flow quality | 10 |
| Valuation | 10 |
| Management/governance | 5 |
| Competitive moat | 5 |
| **Total** | **100** |

Interpretation:

- 80–100: Excellent candidate
- 70–79: Good candidate
- 60–69: Needs deeper research
- <60: Avoid unless a special thesis exists

### Missing or unavailable inputs

Unavailable inputs must be identified as unavailable, not treated as zero-valued evidence that passes a check. Do not substitute a fixed industry P/E or PEG when those values are unavailable. A Priority 10 check with missing inputs does not pass and receives no points; the overall quality score remains a provisional score until required data is available. A provisional score is not, by itself, a buy signal.

The Opportunities screen may show an industry-relative P/E reference when both company P/E and an industry P/E are available. This comparison is not an intrinsic fair-value estimate or a guaranteed target price; if either input is missing or stale, show valuation as unavailable.

## 5. Advanced analysis

### Moat

- Market share
- Market-share trend
- Pricing power
- Switching cost
- Network effect
- Brand strength

### Growth quality

- Revenue growth vs industry
- Profit growth vs revenue
- Margin trend
- New-product contribution
- Capacity utilization

### Cash quality

- CFO/PAT, ideally >1 over time
- FCF conversion
- Working-capital control
- Receivable days
- Inventory days

### Balance sheet

- Net debt/EBITDA
- Debt trend
- Interest coverage
- Contingent liabilities

### Management

- Promoter holding trend
- Promoter pledge
- Related-party transactions
- Capital allocation
- ROIC on acquisitions
- Dividend/buyback discipline

### Innovation

- R&D intensity
- Patents/IP where relevant
- AI disruption risk
- Technology adoption

### Future

- TAM
- Industry growth
- Export opportunity
- Regulatory tailwind
- Demographic tailwind

### Risk

- Customer concentration
- Supplier concentration
- Commodity dependence
- Cyclicality
- Governance risk

### Market

- Relative strength
- Institutional accumulation
- 200-DMA
- Volume trend

## 6. AI disruption

Every company receives:

### GREEN — AI beneficiary

AI increases productivity or revenue.

### YELLOW — AI neutral

AI improves efficiency but does not fundamentally replace the core business.

### RED — AI vulnerable

AI can replace a major part of the company's primary service/product.

The classification must include evidence/reasoning and a confidence score.

## 7. Investment attractiveness

Conceptual formula:

`Quality × Growth × Moat × Future Potential ÷ Valuation = Investment Attractiveness`

Do not use this formula blindly. Each component must be normalized before calculation.
